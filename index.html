Stage 1, Diagnosis: Is There a Problem Paid Ads Can Actually Solve?

Before any ad is designed, Sigma documents the business, audience, offer, sales process, and economics. The diagnosis below is assumed for demonstration. In a real engagement, every point would be checked with the client and their data first, because not every business has the same problem.

01

Business & Offer

Assumed: Zuri sells a ₦28,500 "Clear Skin Routine" bundle (cleanser, serum, SPF) through Instagram and WhatsApp. To validate: whether customers buy the full bundle or single items, and how competing brands present their offers.

02

Audience & Buying Motivation

Assumed buyer: men and women aged 20 to 35 in Lagos and Abuja, frustrated by breakouts and dark marks and wary of products that failed before. A trigger might be an event, a new job, or a bad skin week. To validate: Zuri's customer chats and order history.

03

Objections to Overcome

Likely objections: "Will this work on my skin?", "Is it NAFDAC-registered?", "Will it actually arrive?", and "I've wasted money before." Proof, demonstration, and clear terms must answer these before anyone is asked to pay.

04

Economics & Capacity

Illustrative: a ₦28,500 order with about ₦12,500 in product, packaging, and delivery cost leaves roughly ₦16,000 gross profit before ads. Sigma would also confirm stock, delivery capacity, and WhatsApp reply speed, because more leads cannot fix slow follow-up.

The Strategy: Objective, Structure, Tracking, and Budget

Sigma follows one process on every account: diagnose, develop the strategy and offer, establish tracking, develop creative, launch, monitor, optimize and refresh, scale or reallocate, then report and improve. We draw on direct-response principles associated with Sabri Suby and economics-aware principles associated with Alex Hormozi. Sigma's own contribution is how it applies them: diagnosis, testing discipline, evidence-based decisions, and clear reporting.

01

Objective, Offer & Rationale

Objective: profitable first orders from cold Meta audiences. Rationale: Zuri's buyers already chat with brands on WhatsApp, and the purchase is affordable, so a short path suits it better than a long funnel. The offer to sharpen is a free skin-type check before purchase.

  • One objective: paid orders
  • Offer: bundle plus a WhatsApp skin-type check
  • Simple path first; a longer funnel only if the data demands it
02

Meta Structure & Conversion Journey

Two simple campaigns on Facebook and Instagram: prospecting with different creative angles, and retargeting people who engaged or messaged but did not buy. The journey runs from ad to WhatsApp, skin-type check, order, payment, then delivery follow-up.

  • Campaign A: prospecting (cold audiences)
  • Campaign B: retargeting (engaged, not yet bought)
  • Click-to-WhatsApp with a scripted first reply
03

Tracking, KPIs & Reporting

Platform numbers are not the same as sales. Sigma would track WhatsApp conversations started, mark which are qualified, and reconcile orders and refunds against Zuri's own records. Attribution will be imperfect, and the report says so.

  • Order log with a source tag, plus Pixel and Conversions API if a website is used
  • KPIs: cost per qualified conversation, cost per order, conversation-to-order rate
  • Assumed target: cost per order of ₦8,000 or less (break-even near ₦16,000)
  • Implied: about ₦2,000 per qualified conversation if 1 in 4 become orders (to validate)
  • Weekly plain-language report: what happened, what we learned, what changes next
04

Hypothetical Budget & Allocation

Illustrative: ₦450,000 over four weeks, about ₦16,000 a day. The reasoning is enough spend to run several creative tests, but capped so the risk is known before any scaling decision. It is a planning figure, not a forecast of orders.

  • 40% (₦180,000): prospecting with the strongest concepts
  • 35% (₦157,500): creative testing
  • 15% (₦67,500): retargeting
  • 10% (₦45,000): reserve to reallocate on evidence

Creative Concepts, Each With a Job

Creative is a performance variable, not decoration. Each concept below speaks to a different reason a customer might respond, so the test teaches us something instead of showing the same message six ways. These are proposed concepts, not live ads.

• Problem-Led: "Breakouts Again?"

Hook: the frustration of a breakout before an important week. Format: 15-second video. CTA: "Message us for a free skin-type check."

• Demonstration: The 3-Step Routine

Hook: "Here's exactly what goes on your face, morning and night." Format: short demo video or carousel. CTA: "Message us to get the routine."

• Objection-Led: "Will It Work on My Skin?"

Headline: "Not sure of your skin type? We check first." Format: static image with a clear text overlay. Copy angle: remove the risk before asking for payment.

• Proof: Real Customer Experience

Only genuine reviews and photos used with permission. None are supplied in this example, so this concept stays on hold until real material exists. Format: testimonial video or screenshot carousel.

• Founder-Led: Why Zuri Exists

Hook: a founder explaining why they started the brand. Format: talking to camera. Copy angle: build trust with a sceptical audience. The story would come from the real founder, not a script we invent.

• Offer-Led: The Starter Bundle

Headline: "The 3-step routine, delivered in Lagos." Format: static or carousel. Copy angle: price, what's included, and delivery terms. Tested against the others, not assumed to be best.

Testing: What We'd Test First, and What We Wouldn't Conclude

Each test has one clear hypothesis and a decision attached. With a modest budget, results are often directional rather than statistically decisive, and Sigma says so instead of pretending the data proves more than it does.

Hypotheses Worth Testing First:

  • ✓ A product demonstration beats a static image for cold audiences, judged on cost per qualified conversation, not clicks.
  • ✓ Objection-led copy ("we check your skin type first") brings more qualified WhatsApp conversations than outcome-led copy.
  • ✓ An idea from Zuri's own organic posts, such as its most-asked question, can be adapted into a paid ad that works on cold audiences.
  • ✓ A scripted WhatsApp first reply turns more conversations into orders than an unscripted one.

NOT Proof, On Their Own:

  • ✕ Organic likes and comments. Organic followers differ from cold audiences, so organic content is a source of ideas, not proof of paid performance.
  • ✕ Cheap clicks or a low cost per conversation when few of those conversations become orders.
  • ✕ A single good or bad day, or a test with too little data to separate luck from signal.
  • ✕ A result where several things changed at once, unless we state that we cannot tell which change caused it.

Scaling: Monitor, Optimize, and Decide With Evidence

After launch, Sigma reviews performance at both the ad level and the business level. The criteria below are illustrative starting points for Zuri, not universal rules. Real thresholds depend on the data, the economics, the budget, and the client's risk tolerance.

Monitor & Learn

Quick checks for delivery problems, with weekly reviews for actual decisions.

  • Delivery, spend, and creative response
  • Qualified conversations and orders checked against Zuri's records
  • Feedback from Zuri's WhatsApp sales chats

Optimize & Refresh

Creative is refreshed when evidence points to fatigue, not on a fixed calendar date.

  • Watch for frequency rising while cost per order worsens
  • Check tracking, offer, and follow-up before blaming the ad
  • Replace with genuinely new concepts, not recoloured copies

Scale, Reallocate & Report

Spend rises only when the economics and the evidence support it.

  • Increase gradually once cost per order holds below target on enough orders to trust
  • Hold or cap spend if Zuri's stock or reply speed cannot cope
  • Report what changed, what was learned, and what happens next

Assumptions, Risks & Responsibilities

What this sample strategy depends on, what could go wrong, and what the client needs to do. Every business is different, so these answers describe this fictional example.

Is Zuri Skin Co. a real client? +

No. Zuri Skin Co. is a fictional business created to demonstrate Sigma's method. It is not a case study, and nothing on this page is evidence of past client work or results. The budget, margins, conversion rates, and targets are illustrative assumptions, and the campaign has not been launched.

What assumptions does this strategy depend on? +

That customers will buy at ₦28,500, that roughly one in four qualified WhatsApp conversations becomes an order, that margins and delivery costs are close to those assumed, and that Zuri has stock and replies quickly. Each would be checked against real data before launch, and the plan changes if they do not hold.

What are the main risks? +

A weak offer or economics, slow WhatsApp follow-up, tracking that under-reports sales, a small budget that gives only directional learning, and creative that does not resonate. Sigma would name these in the strategy and plan how to spot them early.

What would Zuri need to do? +

Reply to enquiries quickly, share order and sales-status data, keep stock and delivery reliable, approve creative promptly, give access to the ad and business accounts, and supply real reviews and photos with permission. The campaign's success depends on these as much as on the ads.

Is ₦450,000 the right ad spend budget? +

It is an illustrative figure chosen for this example. For a real client, Sigma sets the starting budget from the product price, the target cost per customer, and how much risk the client is comfortable with, and reviews it as evidence comes in.

Why WhatsApp instead of a landing page for this example? +

Zuri's buyers already talk to brands on WhatsApp, and the purchase is affordable, so a short path suits it. Another business might do better with a landing page or booking flow. Sigma chooses the path from how that business's customers actually buy, not from a default template.

Do you guarantee sales or ROAS? +

No. Results depend on demand, the offer, reply speed, fulfillment, and ad-platform auction dynamics. What Sigma controls is the process: clear hypotheses, honest measurement, and evidence-based decisions about optimizing, pausing, or scaling.

What if the economics don't work? +

Then Sigma says so and recommends what to fix first, such as the offer, pricing, or follow-up, instead of hiding weak fundamentals behind impressive-looking ads.

How much does working with Sigma cost? +

Management fees depend on scope, the channels involved, campaign complexity, and how much ongoing management your business needs. We quote this transparently after an initial conversation about your business. Your ad spend is paid directly to Meta, TikTok, or Google and is always separate from our fee.
